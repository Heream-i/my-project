Building Control Decisions and the Right of Appeal: The UK Statutory Position


Published as an independent commentary and public interest review revealing local authority building control negligence under primary UK law.

When a local authority or a registered building control approver issues a decision regarding building regulations, specific primary legislation dictates who possesses the legal right to challenge it. The regulatory framework, governed primarily by the Building Act 1984 and amended by the Building Safety Act 2022, establishes distinct boundaries for different stakeholders.

1. The Statutory Framework

The UK building control system limits administrative and statutory rights of appeal to specific parties.

  • Statutory Restriction: The Building Act 1984 limits the right of administrative appeal strictly to the person who submitted the plans or the person executing the work.
  • Third-Party Standing: Neighbours, local citizens, and members of the public have no legal standing within the statutory building control appeals framework.
  • Finality of Approval: If a local authority approves an application that contains a technical error or non-compliance, there is no direct statutory or administrative mechanism for a third party to overturn that approval.

2. Legal Positions by Stakeholder

Applicants & Property Owners

Status: The direct party to the application.
Statutory Rights: Entitled to challenge a local authority's rejection of plans, refusal to grant a completion certificate, or the issuance of enforcement notices.
Recourse: Formal appeals are directed to the Building Safety Regulator (BSR) or the First-tier Tribunal within defined statutory timeframes (typically 28 days for enforcement notices).

Developers & Contractors

Status: The commercial entity executing the building works or depositing the plans.
Statutory Rights: Holds full rights to challenge building control rejections, compliance notices, or stop notices.
Recourse: For standard projects, challenges proceed via the BSR. For higher-risk residential buildings, the process follows the specialized BSR building control regime.

Neighbours & Third Parties

Status: Members of the public or owners of adjacent properties affected by the development.
Statutory Rights: Possess zero rights of appeal within the building control framework against either an approval or a rejection.
Recourse: Administrative appeals cannot be lodged. The sole avenue to contest an invalid approval is a Judicial Review in the High Court, which examines the legality of the local authority's decision-making process rather than the technical design merits. This process is subject to a strict court application deadline (typically six weeks from the decision date).
